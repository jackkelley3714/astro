The NFL spent years planning its first regular-season game in France. The Pittsburgh Steelers and New Orleans Saints kick off Sunday at Stade de France, 9:30 a.m. ET. The game is still on. Nearly everything around it is not.

## The Fan Experience, Now Featuring Fewer Fan Experiences

Here is the timeline, and I promise I am not making it up.

On Thursday, the Steelers canceled their fan party in Paris's Bastille neighborhood after unrest nearby. On Friday, the league closed its pop-up shop. On Saturday, it closed NFL House, which had a schedule of events for French athletes and community programs. The Saints, ever diplomatic, postponed Saints House until Sunday, which is a polite way of saying "the party is now on the day of the game." The league also pointed shoppers to a replacement store at Stade de France, so at least the merchandise survived. [ESPN, NBC News/AP, Newsweek]

Priorities, folks.

## Communications Strategy: Check the Facebook Group

The NFL announced the NFL House closure through its OnePass app. Meanwhile, AP reported that one Steelers fan stayed away from the protests thanks to advice in fan-run Facebook groups. The league has the app, but the fans have the intelligence network. [NBC News/AP]

## Soccer Moves, Football Stays

Paris Saint-Germain's match against Lyon was pushed from Sunday to Monday night at the request of Paris police, who have a lot on their plate. The NFL told Fox News Digital that the PSG decision did not affect its plans for the Steelers-Saints game. [Fox News/OutKick, Sky Sports]

So one of France's biggest sporting institutions got rescheduled, and the American import did not. Soft power works in strange ways.

## Everyone Has Takes

Trump weighed in on Friday, saying the NFL "could've probably postponed" the game. [The Hill] The league's Peter O'Reilly has said the game was never considered for a different location, and the NFL has insisted all week that it will be safe to play and attend. [Newsweek, Yahoo Sports]

Roger Goodell, Saints owner Gayle Benson and Steelers president Art Rooney II also met French President Emmanuel Macron at the Élysée Palace on Friday to talk about the game and the NFL's long-term future in Paris. [ESPN] That meeting was probably not on the original itinerary.

## The Irony Nobody Scheduled

The Saints were picked as the host team because of New Orleans' deep French roots, from the fleur-de-lis on the helmet to the Louisiana Purchase of 1803. [NBC News] It made perfect historical sense. The State Department alert telling Americans in Paris for the NFL game to be aware of their surroundings was not part of the heritage tour. [ESPN]

## Before You Laugh Too Hard

The jokes above are about the league's logistics, not the people involved. The protests are reportedly driven by concerns over living costs and school conditions, and some have turned violent, with curfews in parts of France. [Newsweek] More than 70,000 police are reportedly deployed across the country. [The Comeback, citing the New York Times] Goodell has said street protests are not unusual in France.

Hopefully, as one outlet put it, the protests stay peaceful and the game goes off without a hitch. If it does, the NFL's French debut will have come with the most expensive stress test in league history. If it doesn't, we will be writing a very different post.

Kickoff is at 9:30 a.m. ET.
